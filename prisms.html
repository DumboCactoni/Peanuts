Jiashen Li's Research Proposal – 2026 Dear Members of the Research Committee, I am writing to respectfully request your consideration of the enclosed research proposal. The project explores the optimization of magnetic stirring rod geometry for efficient fluid mixing, with the aim of contributing meaningful insights to applied physics. I believe the proposal meets the criteria for rigorous academic inquiry and hope you will find it worthy of your review.

I. Proposal Highlights Area of Study: Applied Physics Topic: Improved magnetic stirring Research Question: How can the vortex formed based on the geometry of a magnetic stirring rod be modelled to determine stirring efficiency? Research Plan:

  • Characterize the vortex formed by standard stirring rods in standard lab apparatus today, at different speeds
  • Characterize non-cylindrical stirring rod models designed by models, determining relevant parameters for stirring efficiency
  • Attempt to create and procure an optimum stirring rod based on different conditions of stirring efficiency
  • Determine the practical applications of such a stirring rod, whether it can be more useful at a similar cost to stirring rods today

II. Detailed Proposal I plan to characterize the vortex formed at different stirring speeds with different standard stirring rods and common lab apparatus like beakers and flasks first. Then, experiment with custom non-cylindrical stirring rods designed by others, characterize relevant parameters that improve stirring efficiency. Third, will be to create an optimum stirring rod based on different considerations: one that has the highest maximuum stirring efficiency, when the speed is set highest for example, one that behaves consistently regardless of rotational speed, or probably most useful one that consistently varies stirring efficiency based on rotational speed. Other factors to optimize can include a smooth stirring process, that avoids disrupting or breaking the liquid. Forth, is to procure and produce a few hypothetical stirring rods, along with conducting further experimental testing to find which may maximize stirring efficiency based on different considerations. Lastly, is to determine the practical applications of a new stirring rod. Is there a standard model that can generally be useful, and that can be manufactured at a similar cost to current ones? Also, to be determined are the applications in industry, where it may be more useful for larger stirrers to be custom-designed.

Current work Anecdotally, when I used magnetic stirring rods, visually, they seemed somewhat ineffective at stirring in the shortest time. Currently, there is a US patent for a better designed stirring rod. However, I plan to compare and model several possible designs and different considerations of stirring efficiency in order to find an optimum one. There are studies for the vortices formed by stirring rods, with different models: for example considering only solid body rotation vs a velocity profile, with some considering viscous effects at low rotational speeds. I plan to consider all of these models to derive a useful model for a conventional stirrer in differently shaped fluid containers at different speeds, then to extend that to different stirrer shapes, along with using experimental results.

Applications Industrial applications seem promising, in addition to applications in the lab. If successful and based on the results, there could be a plethora of stirrers optimized for different functions: consistent stirring efficiency regardless of rotation speed, the greatest stirring efficiency at the highest rotation speed, a smooth variable stirring efficiency based on rotation speed, and also a gentle stirrer for example. It is also possible there is one jack of all trades stirrer that seems promising for most applications. Industrial applications may see this on a larger scale, but with the same underlying principles.

Risks There are very few risks. Magnetic stirrers at high speeds may have somewhat sharp ends and may hurt if contacted at those high speeds, but not having sharp edges is a factor in considering an optimum stirrer, which will probably not have them.

Repurposing GLP-1 medicines as a systemic anti-tumor remedy Background GLP-1 agonists naturally produced in the intestine, pancreas and brainstem are prescribed medicines to manage type 2 diabetes, and obesity. They enhance insulin production, suppress glucagon release, reduce appetite and promote weight loss. They reduce inflammation, by inhibiting the NF-KB pathway, which usually triggers it to fight off infection. Protracted activity however promotes cancer cell survival and metastasis when apoptotic checkpoints are suppressed. GLP-1s shift macrophage polarization toward an antitumor M1 phenotype, and inhibit the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway that causes tumor cell proliferation.

When GLP-1 binds its receptor, it activates PKA, suppressing the ERK growth signal and lowering cyclin D1 that drives cell division. The PKA pathway activates AMPK, which shuts down the mTOR growth pathway. p38 pushes cancer cells towards apoptosis, and EGFR-STAT3 which tumors rely on.

Clinical evidence supports that GLP1s reduced cancer risk by 17%, and reduced colorectal cancer risk by 46% compared to insulin. In a mouse study, breast tumor sizes were reduced by half, due to NF-kB inhibition. Reducing inflammation also lowers the buildup of amyloid plaques in the brain and reduces neural damage from oxidative stress, presenting a 40%, or as high as 70%, reduction of dementia and Alzheimer's risk.

In atherosclerosis, chronic inflammation in the arteries, liraglutide slowed down plaque buildup in mice by blocking vessel wall inflammation. In mice with NASH, a liver cancer causing disease, liraglutide reduces liver inflammation, buildup and cell damage.

Methods and budget We developed a long-acting GLP++ optimized for increased receptor affinity and half-life. Carcinoma cells were injected into 60 mice randomly placed in 3 groups of control, GLP++ and exendin-4, which was injected daily for 28 days. Drug development for GLP++ took 153 million dollars, and bringing this promising drug to market may take 1.3 billion, with expected profits in the hundreds of billions.

Estimated results and future work We see a statistically significant improvement with GLP++. At the end of 28 days, the GLP++ performed 42% better than the current exendin-4 drug, and a 60% improvement from the control. Future work should focus on expanding the approach to a longitudinal study in real persons, and coming up with different formulations of GLP most suited to specific cancers and ailments that activate or inhibit specific reaction pathways.

Alka-Seltzer tablets contain citric acid and sodium bicarbonate, along with insoluble substances. The goal of the lab was to determine the unknown mass percent of sodium bicarbonate and citric acid in such a Seltzer tablet, using a graduated cylinder, 2 beakers, a spatulla, mass balance, along with 1M hydrochloric acid and 3g of sodium bicarbonate.

To determine the mass percent of sodium bicarbonate, we aim to add acid in excess such that all of sodium bicarbonate forms sodium chloride, along with water and carbon dioxide, which escapes. The mass of the escaped gas can be computed as a change in mass, and using the molar ratio of sodium bicarbonate with carbon dioxide of 1:1, along with the respective molar ratios, the mass percent of sodium bicarbonate can be computed. reviews

The article is titled Vortex flow generated by a magnetic stirrer.

The paper provides a good foundation for the basic theory behind the mechanism. It introduces the rankine and burgers vortex, which is a solution to an ideal navier-stokes equation. It maps the height of the vortex and finds it strictly to be a function of rotational frequency squared, mostly independent of container shape and stirring bar shape. Very helpfully, it provides derivations for vertex depth, halfwidth of the vortex, allowing for characterizing the velocity using the burgers model. What warrants further review is how especially velocity affects stirring efficiency, which they gloss over by showing a dye pattern, and time to stir. Particularly, at the center of the vortex is a chaotic downward pull. Doing so would require experimentation, probably with a somewhat insoluble solute so it can be better observed, and further modelling.

The article is titled Optimizing recording speed and interrogation window for rotating flow recorded in the ambient light: PIV analysis. It reviews the effectiveness of a phone using PIVLab software, instead of high-speed cameras, specialized synchronizers and high-power lasers. Promisingly, it references the first article I read constantly, and has very specific steps for using PIVLab. Unsurprisingly, high frame rate and resolutions are still preferred, preferably >=240fps importantly, the minimum given the rotational speed to obtain meaningful data. Another point of consideration is the interrogation window size, where particles inside are assumed to have the same local velocity. Smaller windows give better results, but may also include outliers. In general, the results show a very promising fit, though unsurprisingly poorer data for low velocities, which can be remedied by adjusting the interrogation window and using higher frame rate/resolution cameras, so a smartphone camera or generic camera is sufficient.

he paper, numerical and analytical analyses of a tornado model is helpful for deriving the earlier mentioned Burgers vortex. The math is intimidating, but it covers the process thoroughly from the base navier stokes. Then, it uses cylindrical coordinates and backsolves and proves that Burgers must be one solution to the set of differential equations. Again, I have to review the math again, as it is not possible within a week, or probably a month to fully understand this from a relatively low baseline in differential and multivariate fluid dynamics. More importantly, this ties back to the main goal of the project. I should probably try to understand this paper in due course, but mainly, the novelty is not in the math and modelling which is already done. The goal is to collect good data, particularly with the help of computer simulations and prior research with which most of the tedious math, like in this paper, can be manageable, to characterize stirrers on the market, and importantly obtain an optimal stirrer under different conditions.

The article is titled shape optimization (or optimisation for Lord Maximilian because yes he's British) of stirring rods for mixing binary fluids. Yet again the math is mind boggling, but it uses the same flow equations in a different form, and a penalization model, using an adjoint system. Most of the math addresses characterizing the stirrer so that the change in efficiency can be determined from a small change in variable. This gets very complex as you have to manipulate coordinate systems, and is a large chunk of the paper. It also tries to determine the relative shares of diffusive and convective mixing. I was very confused over a mask function, but this quite literally tells you whether you're looking at the fluid... or the mixer (literally can be 0 or 1). The stirrer also has to be untwisted, as optimization leans towards creating a fake stirrer, that pushes stirrer thickness to zero. The latter half of the paper considers using 2 stirrers, or even 5 stirrers, which is not relevant to my project. Fortunately, and hopefully, I can directly use the derived results seen so far, and use a mix of vibe-coding and my own limited Python skills to numerically solve any math that is necessary.   1. Abstract (193 words; the limit is 150–200) Magnetic stir bars are standard lab equipment, but the common pill-shaped bar can be slow, especially for viscous mixtures, polymers and binders, and poorly soluble solids near saturation, where stirring can take hours. Prior work models the stir-bar vortex, measures its velocity field cheaply with smartphone particle image velocimetry (PIV), and uses shape optimization to find stirrers that mix significantly better, but little links the vortex directly to time to stir or applies optimized shapes to standard stir bars. This project will (1) build a Burgers-vortex model linking bar geometry, speed and container size to the 3D velocity field and time to stir; (2) validate it with smartphone PIV and timed mixing tests on 15 solid–liquid and liquid–liquid combinations; (3) extend it to commercial non-cylindrical bars; and (4) use penalization-based shape optimization to design 3D-printed attachments that clip onto standard bars. Targets are time-to-stir predictions within 5% for standard bars and 10% for complex shapes, and an attachment that cuts stir time by 50%, lasts 100 hours and costs under $2. Faster, more controllable stirring would save hours on slow lab preparations, and at industrial scale small efficiency gains mean significant cost savings.

  2. Literature Review and Research Gap

Previous work: The stir-bar vortex follows Rankine and Burgers models, and its depth scales with frequency squared [Halász et al., 2007] The Burgers vortex can be derived from Navier–Stokes in cylindrical coordinates [Schmitt, 1999] Phone video plus PIVlab measures rotating flow accurately [Shah et al., 2022] Adjoint and penalization optimization finds better-mixing stirrer shapes [Eggl & Schmid, 2020] Gap: efficiency is shown only qualitatively (dye patterns). No model links shape → velocity field → time to stir for real mixtures (viscous, near-saturated, clumping). Optimization hasn’t been applied to an attachment for a standard bar on the beaker floor. Significance: saves hours on the slowest mixes, offers controllable and gentle options, costs under $2, works with existing stir plates, and at industrial scale small gains mean significant cost savings
