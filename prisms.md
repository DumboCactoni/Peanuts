Jiashen Li's Research Proposal – 2026
Dear Members of the Research Committee,
I am writing to respectfully request your consideration of the enclosed research proposal. The project explores the optimization of magnetic stirring rod geometry for efficient fluid mixing, with the aim of contributing meaningful insights to applied physics. I believe the proposal meets the criteria for rigorous academic inquiry and hope you will find it worthy of your review.

I. Proposal Highlights
Area of Study: Applied Physics
Topic: Improved magnetic stirring
Research Question: How can the vortex formed based on the geometry of a magnetic stirring rod be modelled to determine stirring efficiency?
Research Plan:
- Characterize the vortex formed by standard stirring rods in standard lab apparatus today, at different speeds
- Characterize non-cylindrical stirring rod models designed by models, determining relevant parameters for stirring efficiency
- Attempt to create and procure an optimum stirring rod based on different conditions of stirring efficiency
- Determine the practical applications of such a stirring rod, whether it can be more useful at a similar cost to stirring rods today


II. Detailed Proposal
I plan to characterize the vortex formed at different stirring speeds with different standard stirring rods and common lab apparatus like beakers and flasks first. Then, experiment with custom non-cylindrical stirring rods designed by others, characterize relevant parameters that improve stirring efficiency. Third, will be to create an optimum stirring rod based on different considerations: one that has the highest maximuum stirring efficiency, when the speed is set highest for example, one that behaves consistently regardless of rotational speed, or probably most useful one that consistently varies stirring efficiency based on rotational speed. Other factors to optimize can include a smooth stirring process, that avoids disrupting or breaking the liquid. Forth, is to procure and produce a few hypothetical stirring rods, along with conducting further experimental testing to find which may maximize stirring efficiency based on different considerations. Lastly, is to determine the practical applications of a new stirring rod. Is there a standard model that can generally be useful, and that can be manufactured at a similar cost to current ones? Also, to be determined are the applications in industry, where it may be more useful for larger stirrers to be custom-designed. 

Current work
Anecdotally, when I used magnetic stirring rods, visually, they seemed somewhat ineffective at stirring in the shortest time. Currently, there is a US patent for a better designed stirring rod. However, I plan to compare and model several possible designs and different considerations of stirring efficiency in order to find an optimum one. There are studies for the vortices formed by stirring rods, with different models: for example considering only solid body rotation vs a velocity profile, with some considering viscous effects at low rotational speeds. I plan to consider all of these models to derive a useful model for a conventional stirrer in differently shaped fluid containers at different speeds, then to extend that to different stirrer shapes, along with using experimental results. 

Applications
Industrial applications seem promising, in addition to applications in the lab. If successful and based on the results, there could be a plethora of stirrers optimized for different functions: consistent stirring efficiency regardless of rotation speed, the greatest stirring efficiency at the highest rotation speed, a smooth variable stirring efficiency based on rotation speed, and also a gentle stirrer for example. It is also possible there is one jack of all trades stirrer that seems promising for most applications. Industrial applications may see this on a larger scale, but with the same underlying principles. 

Risks
There are very few risks. Magnetic stirrers at high speeds may have somewhat sharp ends and may hurt if contacted at those high speeds, but not having sharp edges is a factor in considering an optimum stirrer, which will probably not have them.

How much will it cost?
This largely depends on the cost of procuring custom made magnetic stirrers. Standard ones in chemistry labs are somewhat affordable, along with glass apparatus, coming in at maybe $20. To procure custom stirrers may reach $100, depending on whether they can be 3d printed and on procuring magnets, and a lot on supplier availability. Besides that, standard measurement devices may be used to determine stirring efficiency and speed, and also solutes fit for stirring.

How long will it take?
This is an ambition project fit for 2 years. Modelling the vortices that affect stirring efficiency for standard stirrers, in addition to already designed stirrers, in addition to collecting experimental data is not trivial. Additionally, from this model, determining the perfect stirrer, or even defining the relevant parameters that would describe one is challenging, in addition to procuring a custom stirrer. 

Major checkpoints
First, coming up with an effective model, largely from literature review and experimentation, for existing stirrers in different container shapes. Second, a successful extension of this model to custom stirrers already designed by others, and determining the exact parameters that produce stirring efficiency. Last, would be selecting one, or several optimum stirrers from prototypes by looking at models and experimental data.
