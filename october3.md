I’m a student in [town], and I’m writing to respectfully urge Governor Sherrill to opt New Jersey into the new federal scholarship tax credit before the December 31 deadline.

[One or two sentences about your own school experience: what options you had, or didn’t, and why it mattered.]

Starting in 2027, taxpayers who donate to an approved scholarship organization get a dollar-for-dollar federal tax credit of up to $1,700. The scholarships can pay for tutoring, special-needs services, books, and other school costs for students in public, private, and religious schools. Families earning up to three times the area median income qualify, which covers the typical Princeton household. No state tax dollars are involved, and public school budgets aren’t touched.

Here is the catch: the credit only helps New Jersey students if the governor opts in. If she doesn’t, residents can still claim it, but only by giving to scholarship groups in other states, such as New York, whose governor has said she plans to join.

Princeton shows what’s at stake. With a median household income around $184,000, many residents owe more than enough federal tax to use the full credit. If just 15 percent of Princeton’s roughly 9,000 households gave $1,700, that would be about $2.3 million a year. Without New Jersey opting in, all of it would fund students across state lines.

Meanwhile, the need is right here. Princeton Public Schools has acknowledged persistent gaps for lower-income students, English learners, and students in special education, and it runs extra math and English labs to help. Scholarships could pay for tutoring for those same students, at no cost to the district.

The governor’s office has said it is weighing how the program could benefit public school students. In Princeton, the answer is concrete: math tutoring for a student whose family can’t afford it, or special-needs services for a child who needs them.

Skeptics are right to ask about accountability. Federal law requires scholarship groups to verify family income, spend at least 90 percent of their money on scholarships, and never award scholarships to their own insiders.

Governor Sherrill’s official residence is here in Princeton. As her Princeton-area neighbors, we ask her to say yes and keep our generosity working for New Jersey kids.
